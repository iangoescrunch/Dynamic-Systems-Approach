# Chapter 5: Neural Mechanisms of Developmental Change

Chapter 5 addresses a limitation of the preceding framework: abstract dynamic principles explain general processes of change but remain uninformed about precise mechanisms of attractor stability shifts. Thelen turns to Gerald Edelman's theory of neuronal group selection (TNGS) and Walter Freeman's work on brain dynamics to instantiate developmental processes at neural levels. The theoretical move is demonstrating that the brain itself operates as a dynamic system and that neural selection mechanisms drive developmental change.

## Freeman's Chaos in Olfactory Perception

Freeman's olfaction research reveals perception as macroscopic pattern formation. Recording from 64 sites across rabbit olfactory bulbs, he discovered perceptual identity is carried in spatial amplitude patterns across the entire bulb, not in single neurons.

The critical finding: context dependence. When rabbits learned banana after sawdust training, the sawdust map changed. Representations are dynamic assemblies constituted by global activity rather than fixed structures.

Freeman posits nerve cell assemblies in which Hebbian learning selects connection patterns. Crucially, he identifies chaos in olfactory systems. Perceptual acts are transitions between chaotic attractor basins. Each odorant corresponds to an attractor. When new odorants become meaningful, new attractors are added, and all existing attractors reorganize.

Chaos provides the source of novel activity patterns. The brain actively searches, directs sensory systems through motor commands, and synthesizes perceptions through recursive perception-action cycles.

## Georgopoulos' Population Coding in Motor Control

Georgopoulos' work on cortical reaching control provides complementary evidence. Single-cell activity varies systematically with movement direction, each exhibiting a preferred direction. Cells respond to actual arm movement direction, not target location. Different cells show different preferences distributed throughout three-dimensional space.

Movement direction emerges from population vectors: each cell "votes" by changing activity, and the arm moves in the vector sum direction. Remarkably, population vectors predict movement from neural activity recorded before movement begins. Neither perception nor action arises from localized encoding. Both emerge from collective neural dynamics.

## Edelman's Theory of Neuronal Group Selection

Edelman's TNGS provides the developmental mechanism. The theory integrates neuroanatomy, neuroembryology, and developmental psychology through selectionist principles: molecular processes during embryology, neuronal processes during primary neuroanatomy emergence, and behavioral processes forming perception-action categories.

The selectionist framework contrasts with constructionism. Development does not build structures according to blueprints but operates through generation and selection from wider possibility spaces. Variability generation and selective retention replace prescription.

TNGS instantiates this through three mechanisms. Primary repertoire formation during embryology produces neuroanatomical diversity through cell division, migration, and death, generating far more possibilities than will be retained. Secondary repertoire formation operates through synaptic selection: experience strengthens particular patterns. Reentrant signaling creates recursive mappings between neural groups, enabling coordination without executive controllers.

Neuronal groups function as collective variables. Synaptic selection alters attractor landscapes. Reentrant mappings enable context-dependent flexibility where the same groups participate in different assemblies. Critically, TNGS bridges developmental dichotomies. Innate versus acquired dissolves when primary repertoires provide constraints within which experience selects secondary repertoires.

## Selection Versus Construction

The selection-construction distinction is central. Constructionist theories assume that development builds structures: schemas, programs, and representations. Selectionist theories assume that development operates on existing variability. Enormous diversity is generated at multiple levels. Experience selects among possibilities by stabilizing particular patterns.

If behavior emerges through selection, development requires mechanisms for generating variation and selectively retaining it. Variation emerges from genetic recombination, stochastic processes, exploratory behavior, and neural noise. Selection operates through functional consequences: patterns that achieve goals are stabilized through Hebbian modification and behavioral practice.

The selectionist framework accounts for phenomena that challenge constructionist accounts. Behavioral forms can appear suddenly when conditions favor previously unselected patterns. The same genetic endowment produces diverse outcomes through selection in different contexts. Early behaviors can disappear then reemerge as control parameters shift.

## Perception-Action Cycles as Developmental Engine

Cognition emerges through repeated perception-action-perception cycles, positioning active exploration as the primary developmental mechanism. Infants actively generate variability through movement, observe consequences, and selectively strengthen functional patterns.

The mechanism: motor commands direct sensory exploration. Reafference alerts sensory systems. Sensory responses combine to form gestalts informing subsequent motor commands. This recursive process builds perceptual categories through selection. Infants explore objects multimodally. Patterns proving coherent are strengthened. Categories emerge as stable attractors in perception-action space.

Multimodal exploration is critical because robust categories require coordination across visual, haptic, auditory, and motor systems. This coordination emerges through selection: actions generating coherent cross-modal consequences are retained.

## Variability as Developmental Resource

Chapter 5 repositions variability from error to resource. If development operates through selection, generating variation becomes essential. Without variability, no selection occurs.

Multiple mechanisms generate variability. Cell division and migration are stochastic. Neural noise creates fluctuations, and Hebbian mechanisms amplify them. Exploratory movements generate diverse perception-action mappings. Freeman's chaos provides a neural mechanism: chaotic dynamics ensure that neural collectives never occupy the same state twice, providing a substrate for trial-and-error learning.

The implication is that systems that produce controlled variability are better positioned for learning than systems that minimize fluctuations. Infants' exploratory movements generate the variation space that selective processes require. High variability during transitions reflects systems exploring wider state spaces before selection stabilizes new forms.

## Reconciling Behavioral and Neural Dynamics

Chapter 5's contribution demonstrates coherence between behavioral and neural levels. The same principles that govern behavioral development also govern neural organization.

This reconciliation provides explanatory power. When behavioral patterns shift, we ask what neural selection processes underlie these transitions. When infants show increased variability before acquiring skills, neural systems may be exploring broader possibility spaces through chaotic dynamics.

The framework generates predictions. If neural organization operates through selection from variability, restricting exploration should impair learning. This phenomenon can be observed in the case of Genie, the feral child, restrained and confined from ages 20 months to 13 years, who displayed gross motor function and perception-action processing impaired to that of a 1 to 3-year-old. If categories emerge through perception-action cycles, passive observation should produce weaker formation than active manipulation. If reentrant signaling coordinates groups, disrupting feedback should impair coherent perception.
